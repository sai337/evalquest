# Grow EvalQuest into the evaluator's practice gym

EvalQuest should expand in two directions at once: **from talking agents to acting agents** (new packs where the failure is a tool call with a side effect, such as a credit pull, a deleted database, an email sent to the CEO or a stock bought on a peer agent's say-so), and **from spotting failures to proving evals are trustworthy** (new mechanics for labelling traces, calibrating judges, sizing samples and reading A/B results, all graded deterministically in the browser). The documented incident record of 2025–2026 is dominated by exactly the gaps in today's 48 challenges. One pattern covers most technical incidents: untrusted content arrives through a tool the user asked to read, and a write-capable tool becomes the exfiltration path (Supabase MCP, GitHub MCP, EchoLeak, Comet, Atlas, A2A smuggling). The customer-facing record has moved from bots that said something wrong to bots that did something wrong (BMW Toronto booked a buyback, Who Gives A Crap confirmed a price typo as policy). The skill gap is just as sharp. EvalQuest already teaches pass@k vs pass^k, judge position bias and held-out validation, but it has no challenge on Cohen's kappa, confidence intervals, clustered standard errors, open/axial coding, grader bugs or OWASP Agentic risks, which are the concepts that separate practitioners from SMEs. The interactive market is crowded on the attack side (Gandalf, HackAPrompt, Crucible, Microsoft's labs), and the research found no free, offline, game-style tool that trains the defender's evaluation job, so EvalQuest has a clear "Gandalf for evals" position. The roadmap below adds 16 packs (about 75 challenges) plus about 18 new challenges in existing packs, 11 mechanics, 5 failure codes, a timed Pro exam and an OSCP-style SME capstone, ordered in five phases. Evidence labels used throughout: **[V]** fetched and read by the research team, **[K]** canonical source known but not fetched, **[H]** headline only, **[U]** unverified background knowledge or a designed hypothetical scenario.

## Agents that write to systems of record now dominate the incident record

### Where agentic AI is actually deployed, and how each deployment fails

The best-documented action-taking deployments sit in a handful of domains, and each has a characteristic failure shape that translates directly into a trace. In healthcare administration, MUSC Health runs Notable agents that work inside payer portals and write approved codes into Epic. **40% of prior auths run with no human involvement**, the task fell from about 30 minutes to about 1 minute, and the design rule is explicit: "If it's denied, it goes to a human" ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/ai/prior-authorizations-patient-check-ins-musc-healths-ai-agents.html)) [V]. The US Department of Labor's DOLA agent on Agentforce opens formal cases across **all 28 DOL programs**, is grounded in 2,900+ knowledge articles, and hands off when the citizen asks for a human ([Salesforce IR](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/U-S--Department-of-Labor-Taps-Agentforce-to-Enhance-Citizen-Support/default.aspx)) [V, vendor announcement]. Rocket Mortgage's Sierra-built assistant **pulls credit**, presents personalized rates and claims clients who start with it close at 3x the rate, but the vendor post says nothing about compliance or guardrails ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/ai-agents-in-action-rocket-mortgage)) [V, vendor]. On the technical side, AWS DevOps Agent went GA on March 31, 2026, starts investigating when an alert fires, and reports up to 75% lower MTTR and 94% root-cause accuracy, while leaving undocumented whether remediation needs approval ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/mt/announcing-general-availability-of-aws-devops-agent)) [V, vendor].

Three cross-cutting lessons shape the roadmap. First, **the side effect is the failure**: BMW, Rocket, DOL and Replit all fail through a tool call, so packs must grade the action and the resulting state, not just the text. Second, **results are mostly self-reported**: Salesforce, Sierra, Notable, AWS and Microsoft supplied nearly every success figure above, and two famous reversals were metric failures. Klarna's CEO said cost had been a "too predominant evaluation factor" and "what you end up having is lower quality" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/05/09/klarna-ai-humans-return-on-investment/)), and 11x was reported to claim about $14M ARR against about $3M in post-trial contracts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)). "Audit the vendor's metric" is therefore a teachable skill in its own right. Third, **the agent platform itself fails**, separately from the model: McHire's admin login accepted "123456" and an IDOR exposed **64M+ application chats** ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/123456-password-exposed-chats-for-64-million-mcdonalds-job-applications/)), and a Dialogflow CX flaw let a compromised bot monitor and impersonate others in the same project ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/07/varonis-google-ai-agent-chatbot-security)). The AgentTorture incident list warns that news-driven incidents are "not a representative sample" ([AgentTorture](https://www.agenttorture.com/resources/funny-ai-agent-fails)), so packs should present them as case studies, never as failure rates.

### Modalities add their own failure surfaces

Voice, computer use and multi-agent systems each fail in ways a text trace of a single agent cannot show. τ²-bench's dual-control telecom domain, where the user also acts on a shared environment, drops gpt-4.1 to **34% pass^1 versus 74% on retail**, and moving from no-user to dual-control cuts pass^1 by about 18–25%, which the authors attribute to coordination rather than reasoning ([arXiv 2506.07982](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07982)). OSWorld-Verified lists environment instability, timing, ambiguous instructions and "valid alternative solutions marked wrong" as failure sources, the last being an evaluator bug rather than an agent bug ([XLANG](https://xlang.ai/blog/osworld-verified)). MAST, built from 1,642 annotated multi-agent traces with inter-annotator κ = 0.88, finds the three most common modes are **step repetition (15.7%), reasoning-action mismatch (13.2%) and being unaware of termination conditions (12.4%)** ([arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)). All three are visible in a trace, which makes MAST the natural backbone for a multi-agent pack and a labelling mechanic.

## Five tiers of evaluator skill, from one trace to a defensible 2-point gain

The field has converged on a vocabulary and a workflow: read traces, open-code and axial-code failures, build binary failure-specific evaluators (code first, LLM judge second, humans to calibrate), then add statistics, CI gates and production monitoring. Anthropic's January 2026 guide defines the core terms (task, trial, grader, transcript, outcome, evaluation harness, agent harness) and insists that the outcome is the environment state, "whether a reservation actually exists in the database, not whether the agent claimed one was made" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)) [V]. Husain and Shankar call axial coding "the most important step" and tell practitioners to annotate at least 30 traces themselves before looking at agent suggestions ([Evals FAQ](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)) [V]. The map below is the curriculum EvalQuest should cover; the last column shows today's coverage based on the 48 existing challenges.

Two items in this map deserve special treatment because they are counterintuitive and therefore make good game moments. The pass^k arithmetic: 75% per-trial success gives about **42% pass^3** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)) and, by the same formula, about 10% pass^8 (research-team arithmetic). The grader-forensics stories: CORE-Bench rejected "96.12" when "96.124991…" was expected, and a model "failed" a τ-style flight task by finding a policy loophole that served the user better ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). The common-mistakes list maps one-for-one onto "spot the flaw" challenges: not reading traces, Likert scales, unvalidated judges, rigid path grading, broken tasks (a 0% pass rate usually means a broken task or grader), leaky environments, one-sided datasets, naive SEs and trusting saturated benchmarks ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents); [Evals FAQ](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)). No published competency framework formally defines "pro" versus "SME" evaluator skills, so the tiering above is a synthesis, not a standard.

Several topics remain unresearched and should not enter pack explanations until sourced: nDCG, Fleiss' kappa and Krippendorff's alpha wording; contamination and canary-string methods; voice-agent metrics (WER, time-to-first-audio, barge-in); fairness and PII-leakage eval methods beyond OWASP LLM02; and 2026 feature specifics of tools such as Phoenix, LangSmith, Braintrust, Langfuse, Ragas and DeepEval [K].

### Five failure codes close the taxonomy gap without blurring it

`src/engine/taxonomy.ts` holds ten codes. Each new code must earn its place by teaching a distinct eval, and project rule 1 requires tests first. The proposal adds five codes plus a schema change, `answer.also_accept: [codes]`, so a step that is legitimately both a policy break and an unapproved action does not punish a defensible answer.

| New code | Label and hint | Distinct eval it teaches | Anchor incidents |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unapproved_action` | Unapproved action: takes an irreversible, destructive or binding action without required consent or approval | Approval-gate and blast-radius checks on write tools | Replit DB deletion, Antigravity drive wipe, BMW buyback booking, A2A `buy_stock` ([Unit 42](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/agent-session-smuggling-in-agent2agent-systems/)) |
| `spec_gaming` | Specification gaming: changes its own constraints, tests, limits or grader instead of doing the task | Graders resistant to shortcuts; protected test files | Sakana AI Scientist extended its own timeout ([Slashdot/Ars](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/24/08/14/2047250/research-ai-model-unexpectedly-modified-its-own-code-to-extend-runtime)); existing Patch "edits the test" challenge |
| `coordination_failure` | Coordination failure: ignores, withholds or contradicts another agent's or user's input, or says one thing and does another | MAST FC2-style step diagnostics | MAST reasoning-action mismatch 13.2%, task derailment 7.4% ([arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)); τ²-bench dual-control drop |
| `misrecognition` | Misrecognition: mishears, misreads or misparses the input (speech, screen, document) | Perception-layer evals separate from reasoning | McDonald's/IBM turned "a large water and a cup of ice cream" into a cream packet ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/06/17/mcdonalds-ai-order-taking-drive-thru-ends/)) |
| `unsafe_output` | Unsafe output: emits content that is dangerous when rendered or executed (auto-fetched image URLs, links carrying data, scripts) | Output validation, OWASP LLM05 Improper Output Handling ([OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/)) | EchoLeak Markdown image exfiltration ([arXiv 2509.10540](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10540v1)) |

The research considered and rejected `fabricated_evidence` (fold into `hallucination` with the explanation naming the sub-type), `sycophancy` (fold into `policy_violation`) and `access_control_failure` (platform authz bugs like McHire's IDOR do not appear as an agent step, so they belong in Eval Call items, not Trace Detective). Each new code needs a glossary entry, an engine test that existing packs still validate, and a `DECISIONS.md` line.

### Regulatory claims wait in a verification queue

Policies make the best puzzles, but the statutory rule map in the notes came from background knowledge and was not fetched. Only the case law (Moffatt, Mobley v. Workday's conditional ADEA certification ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/ai-bias-lawsuit-against-workday-reaches-next-stage-as-court-grants-conditional-certification-of-adea-claim)), the nH Predict suit), the NYC local rules cited by The Markup, Queensland's licensed electrical work boundary ([news.com.au](https://www.news.com.au/lifestyle/real-life/news-life/bunnings-responds-after-ai-tool-gave-out-illegal-advice/news-story/3cc477852899cb6d75302f99e0cf9193)), FINRA's 2026 hallucination flag ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/chatbot-tracker/2026/courts-tell-companies-they-own-what-their-chatbot-says/)) and EU AI Act Art. 55 are verified [V]. Everything else is [U] until a primary text is fetched: Reg E, UDAAP, FDCPA/Reg F, ECOA/Reg B, FCRA permissible purpose, RESPA/TILA, HIPAA minimum necessary, CMS-0057-F, California SB 1120, the NAIC AI bulletin, Colorado SB21-169 and SB24-205 (and its 2026 effective date), NYC Local Law 144, the FCC's AI-voice TCPA ruling, FERPA/COPPA, OMB M-24-10/M-25-21, and the EU AI Act Annex III high-risk timeline after the Digital Omnibus proposal. The practical rule for authors: a pack may state a fictional company policy as its rule (the agent's `policies` list) and teach the eval, and it should only name a statute in the explanation once the queue item is verified.

## A five-phase roadmap: 16 packs, 11 mechanics, a Pro exam and an SME capstone

### Phase 0 lays foundations every later phase needs (1–2 weeks)

Five engine changes come first because every new pack depends on them, and each is small. **Provenance**: add a pack-level `sources: [{title, url, verified: bool}]` list and a per-challenge `provenance: documented | benchmark | hypothetical`; a content eval fails any pack without at least one source and any `hypothetical` challenge whose explanation does not say so. **`also_accept`** codes as above. **The five taxonomy codes**, tests first. **Confidence overlay**: an optional 50/70/90/99% confidence pick on every challenge, scored with a Brier score and shown as a calibration curve on the profile, stored in localStorage only. Roediger and Karpicke found restudy inflated confidence without improving delayed retention ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)), so showing players their overconfidence is itself instruction. **Review deck**: missed challenges resurface after 1, 3 and 7 days, offline. Mastery gating replaces XP gating: a tier unlocks at 75% on the tier below, the threshold Agent Breaker uses ([HackerDNA](https://hackerdna.com/blog/gandalf-ai)). None of this needs a backend or analytics, consistent with project rule 6.

### Eleven new mechanics, all graded deterministically in the browser

The design principle that keeps grading deterministic: **for numeric mechanics the engine computes the truth from data in the YAML, and a content eval asserts that the author's declared answer equals the computed value**. Authors cannot ship a wrong answer key, and rule 2 (fix the pack, never weaken the eval) stays enforceable.

| Mechanic (`type`) | Player task | YAML data needed | Deterministic grading rule | Tiers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label Bench (`label_traces`) | Open-code 8–20 mini-traces: mark pass, or the failing step plus code; then pick the top failure mode to fix first | `traces[]` each with `gold: {pass} or {step, code}`; `fix_first` answer | Cohen's kappa between player codes and gold ≥ `target_kappa` (e.g. 0.7) and correct `fix_first` (highest count, ties by severity) | Practitioner, Advanced |
| Held-out Judge (`judge_builder` v2) | Build a judge from visible `train` samples; graded on hidden `test` samples | Existing judge fields plus `split: train\|test` per sample; optional `weights` and `threshold` rule | Engine applies the chosen rule to test samples; pass if TPR ≥ `min_tpr` and TNR ≥ `min_tnr`; content eval asserts a judge that overfits train fails test | Advanced, Pro |
| Judge Calibrator (`judge_calibration`) | Given judge-vs-human labels, enter TPR, TNR, kappa; decide ship, fix or relabel | `matrix: {tp, fp, fn, tn}`, `policy` (e.g. ship if TPR ≥ 0.9 and kappa ≥ 0.6) | Engine computes metrics; numeric answers within ±0.02; decision must match policy applied to computed values | Advanced, Pro |
| Sample Sizer (`sample_size`) | Choose N for a target CI half-width or minimum detectable effect | `baseline_rate`, `target_halfwidth` or `mde`, `alpha`, `power`, `options[]` | Engine computes closed-form binomial N; correct option is the smallest bucket ≥ computed N | Pro |
| A/B Readout (`ab_readout`) | Read two eval runs and call real, noise or need more data | `a: {mean, n}`, `b: {mean, n}`, `paired_corr`, `cluster: {count, icc}` | Engine computes paired-difference CI with cluster adjustment; answer is "real" if CI excludes 0, "noise" if CI excludes the MDE on both sides, else "need more data" | Pro, SME |
| Spot the Eval Bug (`eval_bug`) | Click the buggy line in a judge prompt, grader function or harness config | `code_lines[]`, `answer: {line, bug_type}` with `bug_type` from a short fixed list (leakage, rigid match, wrong denominator, missing isolation, one-sided set) | Exact line and type match | Practitioner, Pro |
| Triage Queue (`rank_queue`) | Order 6–10 production traces by severity and business impact | `items[]` with `gold_rank` | Kendall tau vs gold ≥ 0.6, plus the top item must be correct | Advanced (SRE, SOC packs) |
| Twin Trace (`twin_trace`) | Compare two traces of the same task (agent alone vs with an acting user, or before vs after a model update); find the first divergence and classify it | `trace_a`, `trace_b`, `answer: {step, code, cause: reasoning\|coordination\|regression}` | Exact step, code (or `also_accept`) and cause | Advanced, Pro |
| Red Team Sim (`red_team`) | Assemble an injection from tiles (channel × instruction × disguise) against a rule-based agent; then pick the defence that blocks it without breaking utility tasks | `simulator: {defences[], tools[], goal_state}`, `tiles[]`, `utility_tasks[]`, `defence_options[]` | A finite state machine evaluates the payload; attack scores if `goal_state` is reached; defence scores only if attack fails **and** all utility tasks still pass (AgentDojo's utility-plus-attack rule) | Advanced, Pro |
| Postmortem (`postmortem`) | Multi-stage: first failing step, root-cause code, which regression eval to add, guardrail or evaluator placement | Trace plus four answer keys | Per-stage exact match; partial credit per stage | Pro, SME |
| Suite Architect (`suite_budget`) | Pick evals to cover weighted risks under a dollar, token or labelling-hour budget | `candidates[]` with `cost` and `covers[]`, `risks[]` with `weight`, `budget` | Engine brute-forces the optimum (≤15 candidates); pass if player coverage ≥ 90% of optimal within budget | SME |

### Pro is a timed cert-style exam; SME is an evidence-graded practical

Top-tier credentials split into two models, and EvalQuest should use one for each tier. Google's Professional ML Engineer exam is 2 hours of 50–60 scenario MCQs on role capabilities, and evaluation is not even a standalone section ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/machine-learning-engineer)). OSCP+ is a 23h45m hands-on practical followed by a 24-hour report window in which missing evidence can cost points ([OffSec](https://www.offsec.com/offsec/oscp-exam-guide/)).

**Pro format ("Pro Exam").** A 60-minute timed set of 25 items drawn by a seeded random generator from a Pro pool of at least 75 items across at least 6 packs, mixing Judge Calibrator, Sample Sizer, A/B Readout, Held-out Judge, Twin Trace, Red Team Sim and scenario Eval Calls. Each item carries a mandatory confidence pick. The pass mark is 75% correct **and** a Brier score at or below 0.20, so a player who guesses confidently does not pass. The score report breaks results down by competency (judge validation, statistics, security, production monitoring, reliability) rather than by pack. Unlock requires 75% across Advanced. Content focus is the verified Pro concepts: clustered SEs, paired tests, power, saturation, grader bugs, guardrail placement, online sampling, utility-plus-attack and the OWASP Agentic list. Because the seed is deterministic, a run can be replayed for review.

**SME format ("Capstone").** Three capstone scenarios, each an unseen system with 30–50 traces in a fresh domain (candidates: a prior-auth agent, an MCP-connected data agent and a multi-agent research crew). Stage 1 is Label Bench on the full set. Stage 2 builds a Held-out Judge to a target TPR/TNR. Stage 3 is Suite Architect under a budget. Stage 4 is Sample Sizer plus A/B Readout on a provided before/after run. Stage 5 is a structured eval report: pick-list claims, each of which must cite trace IDs as evidence, mirroring OSCP's evidence rule, plus a governance card that maps the system's exposure to OWASP ASI items and states whether Art. 55-style adversarial testing documentation is needed. Each stage awards partial credit, and an SME badge requires two of three capstones passed. SME also includes "debate cards", short scenario Eval Calls where the right answer depends on product stage, such as when eval-driven development fits (Anthropic) and when to write evaluators only for observed errors (Husain and Shankar).

An honest limitation applies to both tiers. A static offline game ships its answer keys in the bundle, so badges cannot be cheat-proof without a backend. They should be framed as portfolio and learning signals, not accredited credentials. The optional Supabase path can record a signed-in completion with seed and timestamp, which is enough for a shareable GitHub-linked result. The research found no dedicated professional certification for LLM evals to benchmark against, which is the opening.

### Growth tactics backed by evidence, not folklore

The only quantitative launch evidence found, a study of 138 AI/LLM repos on Hacker News, shows average gains of about **121 stars in 24 hours and 289 in 7 days**, with much lower medians. The top predictors are HN score, existing stars and posting hour (12–17 UTC is best, about +200 stars versus poor hours), and the "Show HN" label gave no significant boost after controls ([arXiv 2511.04453](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04453v1)). The implication is to soft-launch first and build baseline stars, then post to HN at the end of Phase 1 when the security packs and Red Team Sim give a strong demo hook, between 12:00 and 17:00 UTC on any weekday. The README should follow the pattern of microsoft/generative-ai-for-beginners, which has about 117k stars with 21 numbered lessons, multiple entry points, MIT licensing and **auto-translation into 50+ languages via GitHub Actions** ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/microsoft-s-free-21-lesson-ai-course-quietly-hit-117k-github-stars)): number the packs as a curriculum, put a playable link and a GIF above the fold, and keep `_template.yaml` plus "good first issue" labels prominent. A single-author lab repo should expect low thousands of stars, not six figures; Microsoft's own red-teaming labs sit at about 2.1k ([GitHub](https://www.github.com/microsoft/AI-Red-Teaming-Playground-Labs)).

The strongest distribution lever is becoming the practice gym for existing courses. Husain and Shankar's Maven course costs $4,500, claims over 5,000 engineers and PMs, and has lessons on error analysis, evaluators, CI/CD, retrieval, safety and judge validation ([Maven](https://maven.com/parlance-labs/evals?promoCode=MAVEN100)). DeepLearning.AI's free "Evaluating AI Agents" course with Arize covers tracing, code and LLM-as-judge evals and production monitoring ([DeepLearning.AI](https://deeplearning.ai/short-courses/evaluating-ai-agents)). A "course companion" page mapping each lesson to 5–10 EvalQuest challenges gives instructors a free homework link. Lakera and HackAPrompt turned crowd data into public datasets and an EMNLP paper ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Lakera/mosscap_prompt_injection/blob/main/README.md); [EMNLP](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~jbg/docs/2023_emnlp_hackaprompt.pdf)), but EvalQuest's rule 6 forbids analytics. The compatible version is strictly opt-in: a player exports their Label Bench labels as JSON, or a signed-in player submits them through Supabase with explicit consent. Both need a `DECISIONS.md` entry before shipping. Pack Forge fits the same loop: its drafting agent should be required to fill `sources` with fetched URLs and set `provenance`, so the new content evals reject an ungrounded pack before a human ever reviews the PR, and the same pipeline can draft translations for human review.

## Conclusion

The research changes what EvalQuest is for. It began as a way to learn to spot agent failures. The evidence says the scarce skill in 2026 is proving that an evaluation can be trusted: that 100% on 11 positives is not a ship signal, that a 2-point gain sits inside a clustered confidence interval, that "no permissions were violated" is the problem, not the defence, and that a grader rejecting 96.12 can make a 95% agent look like a 42% one. Nobody teaches that interactively for free, while attack games multiply. Building the rigor mechanics deterministically, with the engine recomputing every numeric answer key, turns EvalQuest's no-LLM constraint from a limitation into its distinguishing feature: every score is reproducible, which is the lesson the game is teaching.

The second shift is about honesty as a product feature. Vendor metrics, news-driven incident lists and unverified statutes are exactly the evidence an evaluator must learn to discount, so the game should model that discipline in its own content through provenance labels, a verification queue for regulations, and Pack Forge content evals that reject ungrounded scenarios. If EvalQuest ships its packs with sources the way it asks players to ship evals with error bars, it teaches the habit along with the content.
